01

How this runs

Trial Reels, not normal Reels

A Trial Reel is an Instagram option you switch on when you post. It sends the reel only to people who don't follow us. Our own followers never see it.

That's exactly what we want here, for two reasons:

  • This is for strangers. The whole point is reaching women who have never heard of HOLLA. Our existing followers aren't the audience.
  • It keeps our feed clean. We can post the same idea nine times without our followers seeing the same thing nine days in a row and unfollowing us.
  • And every run gets a fresh audience, which is what makes them comparable to each other. Instagram gives us the numbers for each one separately.

You turn it on in the app at the moment of posting. If one goes up as a normal Reel by mistake, that run doesn't count and shouldn't be compared to the others.

One a day, and nine of them

Three base videos × three different hooks = nine reels. One goes up every day, or every second day if that's easier. Any hook can go on any video, so we work through the combinations.

The order matters for one small reason: every video meets every hook exactly once. So at the end we can see which video did best and which hook did best, out of the same nine posts.

Who delivers what

Cindy delivers three edits with no text on them. Not nine. The text layer gets added at upload, which is how three videos turn into nine reels without nine rounds of editing.

So: Cindy makes the three videos clean, and the hook text goes on top afterwards.

What to write down for each run, before the next one goes up: which video, which hook, the date, views, likes, comments, DMs, and sign-ups. The number that decides a winner is comments divided by likes. For reference, the brands we copied: cooldownrunning 1.56, stassh 0.67, shopfirsthealth 0.47. Under 0.3 is a loser.

02

The three videos

Three separate edits, no text on any of them. Each one is a woman using a HOLLA tool at home and the result. No talking in any of them.

  • Video 1 — the curling iron. Waves or curls, start to finish.
  • Video 2 — the blowout. Dryer and brush together, wet to smooth.
  • Video 3 — the brushes. The prep and the smoothing, and the shine at the end.

The one rule for all three

The tool is visible and in use in the very first frame. Not a logo, not a face, not a title card. The tool doing its job. Every strong reel we studied does this and every weak one doesn't.

  • Around 15 seconds each, and no longer than 16.
  • Leave the middle section clean so text can sit over it, and keep the top and bottom clear of anything important.
  • Plain edits. No fancy transitions, no zoom punches.
  • The finished hair gets held at the end for two or three seconds.

04

The automation

Use this one

Same automation behind all nine reels. This is @shopfirsthealth's flow, and it's the one to use even though it's the longest of the three we looked at.

Why this one and not the shorter ones

It asks her to post. "Just tag us in your first post or story and we'll take it from there" is the only line in any of the three flows that produces content for us, and content is a large part of why we're doing this at all. @stasshsociety's flow says "no posting required", which is friendlier and gets us nothing to use.

And it captures her email. The other two send her off to a page and lose everyone who doesn't click. The email is the one thing that survives even if she never buys.

It looks like more work than it is: in ManyChat it's four messages and two buttons. The email field is built in, and it pushes to Klaviyo on its own.

Triggers on the word interested in a comment on the reel.
