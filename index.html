How we work — everything ready ahead

Core

The single change I want out of this meeting: nothing gets pulled together at the last minute. Not emails, not social. If it's going out next week, it's finished this week.

The standard

  • Two emails a week. Every week, not "when there's something to say."
  • At least five posts a week.
  • By Tuesday of the week before, next week is done. Not started, not outlined — done. That gives us the rest of the week for my notes and your fixes, instead of scrambling on Friday for a Monday post.
  • I want to know a week ahead what's going out. No surprises, and no approving things the morning they publish.

What "done" has to mean

If we don't define this, I'll get a list of ideas and think I got a finished week. Done = final creative, final caption, email subject line, the link it points to, and the date and time it publishes.

What the numbers say

People open. Nobody clicks.Our open rate matches or beats the benchmark, so the subject lines and the deliverability are fine. Then 2.9% of openers click, against roughly 12%. Whatever is wrong is happening after the email lands and gets opened.

There are two separate problems here and both are real. I don't want us to fix one and assume the other went away.

Problem one — the wiring

  • Most people never get the first email. It should always reach the most people, then fewer at each step. Instead it's 23 → 98 → 80 for the month, and 49 → 152 → 125 over 90 days.
  • My best guess, and it's checkable in five minutes: Smart Sending is switched ON for email 1 and OFF for email 2. Smart Sending skips anyone who has had an email from us recently — so at the highest-intent moment, an hour after they abandoned, most people get skipped, while the generic email that follows always goes out. That is exactly backwards. Please verify it and tell me if I'm wrong.
  • Two live flows share the same name — "Abandoned Cart Reminder · Shopify Added to Cart Trigger" exists twice. Some people may be getting doubled up, and one of the two is losing subscribers at 5%.
  • The first checkout email loses 4% of the people who receive it. For a first touch that is very high.

Problem two — the copy. Here's what I see

92 people received these emails last month, opened them, read them and did nothing. No wiring fix changes that number. So I went through all three emails, and the browse abandonment email that does work, and compared them. This is my read — push back where you disagree.

And here's the one that works — worth copying

Our best-performing email in the whole account is the first browse abandonment email, at $4.35 per recipient — aimed at a colder audience than the checkout emails, and it still beats them. Three things it does differently:

  • The subject line meets the person where they are — "Still Comparing Your Options?" — instead of talking about us.
  • It names the exact thing they looked at, and the main button is "VIEW THE TOOL", pointing straight back at that page.
  • It asks for something small. It isn't trying to close the sale, just to get them back to the page. Low ask, high click.

Both halves of this are yours to own. The copy, and the flows themselves: how they're wired, when they fire, who gets which email, and whether the numbers move afterwards. I want it investigated properly and a short write-up of what you found and what you changed.

The flow we're building instead

I looked up what actually works in these flows rather than just what's wrong with ours. Three things came back consistently:

  • Three emails is the sweet spot. Klaviyo's own analysis: three-email sequences returned $24.9m against $3.8m from single emails. Two extra emails, 6.5× the revenue.
  • The timing that performs: first touch within a couple of hours, second around 24 hours, third at 48–72. Evening sends do best, roughly 7pm in the reader's own timezone.
  • No incentive in the first email. Discounting early teaches people to abandon on purpose and wait for the code. Hold it to the last touch.

⚠ Before we can promise a deadline, one thing has to change

You're right that the incentive needs a real expiry — that's the part we've never had. But we currently have two permanent 10% codes with no end date: HAIRPOWER, and STYLISTPOWER which has been used 302 times and is out in the world. "10% off, expires tomorrow" isn't credible when a 10% code sits on our site forever and one search proves it.

So the flow uses a unique code generated per person by Klaviyo, with a genuine expiry — and we decide what happens to the always-on codes. Otherwise the deadline is a bluff, and it costs us more trust than it earns.

While I was in there I also found a live code, "45678", giving 99% off, with no usage limit and 7 redemptions. Please kill that one today.

Two splits that matter

  • Tools vs Academy. The split already exists in the flow and the Academy side has sent nothing. Digital buyers have completely different objections — lifetime access, what's actually inside, no refund once 20% is watched. Shipping and warranty language is simply false for them.
  • Cart value. Under $100 the strongest lever isn't a discount at all, it's "you're $18 away from free shipping" — honest, specific, and it costs us less than 10%. Save the percentage for carts already over $100.

Rules the copy has to follow

  • One door. Strip the nav, the collection links, the socials and every "Shop now" out of all three.
  • Their item, in every email. Never a bestsellers feed.
  • Never claim something is expiring unless it is.
  • One CTA, repeated, going straight back to their own checkout.
  • Smart Sending off on the first email, and sends in the reader's evening.

How we'll know it worked

  • Revenue per recipient — from $0.75 toward the $3.65 benchmark. This is the number we report.
  • Click-to-open — from 2.9% toward 12%.
  • Recovery rate — a strong 2026 flow recovers 8–12% of abandoned checkouts.
  • Share of recovered orders that used the code. If it's most of them, we're paying for sales we'd have had anyway and we pull the discount back.

Worth deciding: SMS on the first touch. Klaviyo does it, it's the single biggest addition you can make to a cart flow, and it needs us to start collecting phone consent at checkout.

The Reel and its DM funnel

I found a Reel built around a comment-to-DM funnel and I want us to do something in that spirit for HOLLA — not necessarily giving anything away free. You said the copy was the hard part; I have the flow and the copy in mind, so we build this one together.

The reference — @shopfirsthealth

@shopfirsthealth 16.3K likes 7,609 comments 162 shares 7 days old 
Pr opportunity! ✨
- follow us
- comment "interested"
- send this post to our dms

We'll reach out to you! 📦

I went through the whole thing — commented, and followed the DMs all the way to the end. Here is the actual funnel, step by step.

  1. The Reel promises a free PR package. On-screen: "Brand so small — if you send this reel … we will send you a PR package." The caption asks for three things: follow, comment "interested", and send the post to their DMs. Each action buys something different. The comment drives reach, the follow grows the account, and sending the post as a DM opens a conversation — which is what allows them to message you at all.
  2. First automatic DM — no package, an "ambassador team" instead.
    Hello! 🙌 Thanks for reaching out! We'd love to invite you to our First Health Ambassador Team. As a welcome gift, you'll get an extra 40% off on top of our up to 70% off Summer Sale, plus a free gift with your first order. Would you like the code? ✨
    Yes! I'm interested. The reply is a tap, not typing. Almost nobody drops out at a button.
  3. Second DM — the email address.
    Sweet! Also, what's your email? I'll add you to our VIP Ambassador list
    This is the step I'd copy whatever our offer turns out to be. A comment on a Reel becomes an email subscriber, and "VIP Ambassador list" makes handing it over feel like an upgrade rather than a form.
  4. Third DM — the code, on a clock, plus the ambassador terms.
    To get started, you can use code CT40 at checkout for an extra 40% off our Summer Sale. Our website link is in our bio. Code is valid for 72 hrs. Once your order arrives, we'll activate your ambassador account with your own 15% commission code, exclusive discounts, and access to future gifted collab opportunities. Just tag us in your first post or story and we'll take it from there…
    Shop now A 72-hour deadline, and the ambassador status only activates after the order arrives — so you have to buy first. There's a second 15% dangled through a "creator program" once they've reviewed your first post.

Nobody gets a free PR package.The Reel promises a package; the DM hands you a discount code. To become an ambassador you have to place an order. Off one Reel they collect an order, an email address, a tagged post and a new affiliate — and it costs them nothing.

My take

  • It's clever and we should be doing something like it. The give doesn't have to be free product — theirs isn't.
  • But we don't cross the line into clickbait. The gap between what that Reel promises and what the DM actually delivers is exactly where people get annoyed. Whatever we say in the Reel has to be what genuinely arrives in the DM — then anything extra reads as a bonus instead of a swap.
  • This one is aimed at the at-home customer, not our professional audience.
  • I want to run it as a trial Reel first, so we see how it performs before it goes to our followers.
  • I have the flow and the copy in mind — we build it together.
