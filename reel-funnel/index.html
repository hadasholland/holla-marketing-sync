02

What separates the winners from the template

Key

Seven of these ten run the identical script: "PR opportunity — follow us, comment 'interested', send this post to our DMs." They cluster around 0.4 comments per like. The two at the top both broke the script, in different ways.

1 · Tell them they qualify

The best performer says "we are looking for non-influencers and micro influencers." Everyone else says "PR opportunity", which the person with 400 followers reads as not for me. One clause removes the only objection that matters, and it more than triples the response.

For us: a hairstylist with 800 followers is exactly who we want, and she assumes she isn't. Say it out loud.

2 · One specific word, one nameable prize

"Comment CABANA and enjoy a 6-day complimentary stay" beats "comment interested" — twice over. The word is memorable and makes the comment feel like a key rather than a queue ticket. And the prize is a picture in your head, not a category. "A 6-day stay" lands; "a PR package" doesn't.

3 · Two asks, not three

Both leaders asked for follow + comment. Every reel that also demanded "send this post to our DMs" sat in the bottom half. This revises what I told you earlier — I read the third ask as clever, because it opens a messaging window. The numbers say it costs more in drop-off than it earns. Get the comment first; the DM window can be opened by the automation's own reply.

4 · Quiet beats loud

The 1.11 reel opens with "Hopefully reach the right people ✨" — almost apologetic. The 0.05 reel shouts "PR OPPORTUNITY! 💌". On a platform where every third post is shouting, the understated line reads as real.

And one thing nobody paid for.In the comments of the CABANA reel: "@ba.keenia COME & SEE THISSS! YOU'RE A GREAT FIT 💅🏽" — people tag their friends. Every tag is a free impression to exactly the right person. Worth writing the caption so tagging feels natural.

03

The two DM flows I walked myself

@shopfirsthealth — the complete flow

  1. The Reel promises a free PR package. On screen: "Brand so small — if you send this reel … we will send you a PR package."
  2. Automatic DM — no package. An "ambassador team" instead.
    Hello! Thanks for reaching out! We'd love to invite you to our First Health Ambassador Team. As a welcome gift, you'll get an extra 40% off on top of our up to 70% off Summer Sale, plus a free gift with your first order. Would you like the code?
    Yes! I'm interested. A tap, not typing. Almost nobody drops out at a button.
  3. Second DM — the email address.
    Sweet! Also, what's your email? I'll add you to our VIP Ambassador list
    The comment becomes a subscriber. "VIP Ambassador list" makes handing it over feel like an upgrade rather than a form.
  4. Third DM — the code, on a clock, plus the terms.
    Use code CT40 at checkout for an extra 40% off. Link is in our bio. Code is valid for 72 hrs. Once your order arrives, we'll activate your ambassador account with your own 15% commission code, exclusive discounts, and access to future gifted collabs. Just tag us in your first post…
    Shop now A 72-hour deadline, and ambassador status activates only after the order arrives — so you buy first. Off one Reel they collect an order, an email, a tagged post and a new affiliate.

@cocolifestyleresort — what happened when I tried it

⚠ I commented CABANA and nothing came back

I posted the trigger word on their Reel from our account and waited. No automated DM arrived — not in the inbox, not in message requests. Their caption also said "Follow us at @cocolifestyleresort for more", and we didn't follow.

So either the automation is switched off now, or it only fires for people who follow first. Either way they just lost a lead from a reel with 20,800 comments on it.

The rule that comes out of this: ask for the follow, never gate the DM on it. If someone comments, they hear back — full stop. And we test our own trigger from a phone that doesn't follow us before any of this goes live.

04

HOLLA's version — the one real decision

Decide

Here's the honest problem. Every single one of these ten reels offers the same thing: free product, or a path to becoming an ambassador. That's where the pull comes from. And that pull is aimed at creators, not at customers.

You want this aimed at the at-home consumer, without giving product away free, and without crossing into clickbait. All three of those are right — and together they take away the exact thing that makes the format work. So the offer has to be genuinely worth typing a word for, and it has to arrive in the DM within seconds of the comment. Two coherent ways to do that:

Option A — point it at creators. My recommendation.

This is the play the evidence actually supports, and we already decided the offer. In the meeting we agreed to move creator outreach to an ambassador offer — 40% off plus a real commission path, instead of gifting product. That is precisely the offer this format was built to distribute.

Nothing has to be invented. The Reel says we're looking for bridal and event hairstylists — including small accounts — the DM explains the ambassador offer, and the promise matches the delivery exactly. No clickbait, because we're not pretending it's free.

It also feeds two things we need anyway: the European creator bench, and content we can use.

Option B — point it at the home user, with a different pull

Harder, because a home user doesn't want to be an ambassador. It needs something she'd type a word for and that we can hand over instantly: a specific tutorial, a styling guide, a "which tool for your hair" answer. The give has to be real and immediate — not a discount code dressed up as a gift.

If we go here, the Reel has to promise exactly that thing and the DM has to deliver exactly that thing. The moment it becomes "comment WAVES" → "here's 10% off", we're the reel that promised a PR package and sent a coupon.

Decide

05

The rotation — how we test it properly

Your instinct is right: run it as a Trial Reel, again and again. Trial Reels go only to people who don't follow us — which is exactly the audience this is for — and each one gets its own fresh sample. That makes them a genuine test vehicle, not just a re-post.

⚠ But rotate one thing at a time

Three or four variants live at once, each different in video and hook and flow, teaches us nothing — when one wins we won't know which part won. One variable per round. It costs a few days and it's the difference between testing and guessing.

The order to test in

RoundWhat variesWhat stays fixedWhy this order
1 · The words
3 variants, ~6 days		 The caption and the on-screen line. One "you qualify" version, one "specific word + nameable prize" version, one quiet-opener version. Same footage. Same DM flow. The evidence says the wording drives the 30× spread — not the footage. Test the biggest lever first.
2 · The video
3 variants, ~6 days		 The footage and the first three seconds. Round 1's winning caption. Same DM flow. Now we know what to say, we find out what to show while saying it.
3 · The flow
2 variants, ~4 days		 The DM sequence: two asks vs three, where the email capture sits, button replies vs typed. The winning reel from rounds 1 and 2. Only worth optimising once something is reliably driving comments.
  • One variant live at a time, posted every 1–2 days as you said. Three variants is one round and about a week — so we learn something real every week.
  • Vary the footage across rounds anyway. Posting a near-identical video daily reads as repetitive to the platform. Round 1 keeps the footage fixed across its three variants, then it changes.
  • Log every run in one place before the next goes up: which variant, when, views, likes, comments, DMs, clicks, sales. Without the log the rotation becomes noise.
  • Kill fast. Anything under ~0.3 comments per like is a losing variant. Don't nurse it.
06

The automation build

⚠ First: check whether we already have the tool

The Social Media Planner board already has a column called "Keyword / CTA — ManyChat keyword". So ManyChat may already be paid for. Confirm that before buying anything. Metricool can't do comment-to-DM.

What the automation has to do

  • Trigger on the keyword in a comment on a specific Reel — not on any comment, so ordinary conversation doesn't fire it.
  • Reply publicly in the thread too, briefly. Coco's brand replies inside the comment threads, and every reply is another comment on the post. Free reach.
  • Send the DM within seconds, whether or not they follow us. Never gate it on the follow.
  • Use a button reply for the first response, so step one is a tap.
  • Ask for the email as the second message, framed as joining something rather than filling a form. Push it straight to a dedicated Klaviyo list so the follow-up isn't manual.
  • Deliver the thing we promised, exactly as promised, in the message right after.
  • Have a fallback for anyone who replies something unexpected — a human picks it up rather than the thread dying.
  • Test it from a phone that doesn't follow us before it goes live. This is the step Coco appears to have skipped.
07

What we measure

  • Comments per like — the primary read, and the one number that decides which variant wins. Benchmark from these ten: the template gets ~0.4, a good one gets above 1.0.
  • Views from non-followers — the whole point of a Trial Reel. If this isn't climbing, the reach engine isn't working regardless of the comments.
  • Then the funnel: comments → DMs opened → emails captured → link clicks → orders. Each step is a number, and the one that leaks is the one we fix next.
  • Emails captured is the asset that outlives the reel. Even a variant that sells nothing is worth running if it fills the list.
  • Follower growth as a secondary read — we're asking for the follow, so it should move.

One honest caveat: none of the ten reels tells us what any of this sold. Comments and reach are public; revenue isn't. We'll be the only ones who know whether it converts, which is exactly why the log matters from the first run.
